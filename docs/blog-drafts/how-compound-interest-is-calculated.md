# How Compound Interest Is Calculated

Compound interest earns interest on the original principal and on interest already added to the balance.

## The compound interest formula

For a single deposit, the future value is `A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)`. `P` is the starting principal, `r` is the annual rate as a decimal, `n` is the number of compounding periods per year, and `t` is time in years. Interest earned is `A - P`.

## Example

At 5% annual interest compounded monthly, a $1,000 deposit grows to about $1,051.16 after one year before taxes and fees. Regular deposits require an additional contribution calculation, so enter them separately in the [compound interest calculator](/calculator/compound-interest-calculator).

## Assumptions and limitations

The result assumes a constant rate, regular compounding, and no withdrawals or fees. Bank products may use daily balances, variable rates, or different rounding rules.
